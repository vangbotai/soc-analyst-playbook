# Threat Intelligence Report – Phishing Campaign Analysis

## Overview

Phishing is a social engineering technique used by attackers to trick individuals into revealing sensitive information, downloading malicious files, or visiting fraudulent websites.

Phishing remains one of the most common initial access methods used in cybersecurity attacks because it targets human behavior rather than relying only on technical vulnerabilities.

---

# Attack Method

Common phishing campaigns use:

* Fraudulent emails
* Fake login pages
* Malicious attachments
* Links to compromised websites
* Social engineering tactics

Attackers often impersonate trusted organizations, coworkers, or service providers to increase the likelihood that a victim will interact with the message.

---

# Common Phishing Techniques

## Credential Theft

Attackers may create fake login pages designed to collect:

* Usernames
* Passwords
* Multi-factor authentication codes

## Malicious Attachments

Common malicious attachments include:

* Office documents
* PDF files
* Compressed archives
* Executable files

## Business Email Compromise

Attackers may impersonate executives or vendors to request:

* Financial transfers
* Sensitive information
* Account access

---

# Indicators of Suspicious Activity

Security analysts may look for:

* Unexpected email attachments
* Urgent requests for account information
* Suspicious sender domains
* Spelling or formatting inconsistencies
* Links leading to unknown websites
* Requests to bypass security procedures

---

# Detection Opportunities

Security teams can improve detection by monitoring:

* Suspicious email domains
* Malicious URL reputation
* Attachment analysis
* Authentication anomalies
* Unusual login locations

---

# Mitigation Strategies

Organizations can reduce phishing risk by:

* Providing security awareness training
* Using email filtering solutions
* Enforcing multi-factor authentication
* Verifying unusual requests
* Reporting suspicious messages

---

# Analyst Takeaways

Phishing investigations require analysts to evaluate both technical indicators and human behavior. Understanding attacker techniques helps security teams identify suspicious activity earlier and reduce the impact of successful attacks.
